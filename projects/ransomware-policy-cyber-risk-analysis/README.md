# Ransomware Policy & Cyber Risk Analysis

## Overview

This project examines ransomware as both a cybersecurity threat and a governance, risk, and policy challenge.

Using a fictional Australian health, education, and community care provider as a case study, the analysis considers how ransomware incidents can create operational disruption, data exposure, reputational damage, regulatory challenges, and risks to vulnerable clients.

## Objective

The objective was to evaluate whether governments should prohibit ransomware payments and assess the potential benefits, limitations, enforcement challenges, and unintended consequences of such a policy.

## Key Risks Identified

### Operational Risk
Ransomware can disrupt critical systems and prevent organisations from delivering essential services.

### Privacy Risk
Stolen sensitive information may be exposed or published as part of double-extortion attacks.

### Reputational Risk
Threat actors can use media exposure and public data leaks to increase pressure on victim organisations.

### Regulatory Risk
Organisations must consider legal obligations, breach reporting, privacy requirements, and regulatory involvement during major cyber incidents.

### Financial Risk
Recovery costs, downtime, insurance considerations, and ransom demands can create significant financial pressure.

## Policy Analysis

The project considered the potential advantages of prohibiting ransomware payments, including reducing criminal profitability and discouraging future attacks.

However, several limitations were identified, including:

- difficulties enforcing payment bans
- cross-border cybercrime
- cryptocurrency obfuscation
- potential under-reporting of incidents
- covert ransom payments
- disruption to critical services
- increased harm to victims if stolen data is released

## Recommended Approach

The analysis concluded that ransomware payments should be discouraged, but that an immediate blanket prohibition may create significant practical and enforcement challenges.

A broader harm-reduction approach should include:

- mandatory ransomware incident reporting
- regulatory oversight
- stronger organisational cyber resilience
- multi-factor authentication
- security awareness
- offline backups
- patch management
- public-private intelligence sharing
- international law-enforcement cooperation
- cryptocurrency monitoring and sanctions

## Real-World Cases Considered

The research considered incidents including:

- Medibank
- Colonial Pipeline
- WannaCry

These cases were used to examine data exposure, operational disruption, critical infrastructure risk, victim impact, and ransomware decision-making.

## Key Takeaway

Ransomware is not solely a technical cybersecurity problem. Effective responses require organisations and governments to consider operational, financial, regulatory, reputational, and societal risks alongside technical security controls.
